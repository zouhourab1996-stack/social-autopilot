The BMR math: why any meal-replacement plan can work

Your body burns energy all day. BMR (basal metabolic rate) is what you burn at complete rest. TDEE (total daily energy expenditure) adds your movement, exercise, and digestion. Weight loss happens when you eat less than your TDEE over time.

One widely used way to estimate BMR is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, published in 1990. Here is a worked example for a 35-year-old woman who is 165 cm tall and weighs 70 kg.

Step 1: BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor, women)
10 × 70 + 6.25 × 165 − 5 × 35 − 161
= 700 + 1,031 − 175 − 161 = about 1,395 kcal/day

Step 2: TDEE (lightly active, × 1.375)
1,395 × 1.375 = about 1,920 kcal/day

Step 3: the deficit
If replacing two meals with smoothies brings her total to about 1,450 kcal, her deficit is roughly 470 kcal/day.

What about "8 pounds in one week"?

The testimonials are real people's stories, according to the seller, but the page's own disclaimer says they are not typical results. Here is why fast early drops happen and why they usually slow down:

  • Water and glycogen. When you eat less, especially fewer carbs, your body uses stored glycogen, which is held with water. The first week's drop often includes a lot of water, not only fat.
  • Less food in your gut. Lighter meals mean less food weight in your digestive system.
  • The 3,500-calorie rule overpromises. Researchers at the NIH showed that the classic "3,500 kcal = 1 pound" rule ignores how your body adapts as you lose weight, so it overestimates long-term loss (Hall et al., The Lancet, 2011).

What research says about meal replacements

The good news: replacing meals is a real strategy. A 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis in Obesity Reviews (Astbury et al.) found that programs using meal replacements led to more weight loss at one year than other diets. On average the extra loss was modest (about 1.4 kg), and it was larger when people also got support.

But those studies mostly used nutritionally designed shakes with set calories and protein. Homemade fruit smoothies are not the same thing. Two cautions:

  • Fullness. In a Penn State study, whole apples kept people fuller and cut meal intake more than applesauce or apple juice with the same calories (Flood-Obbagy & Rolls, Appetite, 2009). Blended fruit may leave some people hungrier.
  • Protein. Fruit-based smoothies can be low in protein. Adding Greek yogurt, milk, soy milk, or protein powder helps you stay full and protects muscle while you lose weight.

How to use a smoothie plan safely

  1. Don't drop below your BMR for long without medical supervision. We explain why in Can You Eat Below Your BMR?
  2. Aim for a moderate deficit (often 300–500 kcal/day) rather than a crash.
  3. Build balanced smoothies: a protein source, vegetables (spinach, cucumber), one portion of fruit, and some fiber (oats, chia).
  4. Keep at least one solid meal a day with vegetables and protein.
  5. Plan the exit. Decide in advance how you will eat after day 21 so the weight does not come straight back.
