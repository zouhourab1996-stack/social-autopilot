<p>The good news: replacing meals is a real strategy. A 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis in <em>Obesity Reviews</em> (Astbury et al.) found that programs using meal replacements led to more weight loss at one year than other diets. On average the extra loss was modest (about 1.4 kg), and it was larger when people also got support.</p>

<p>But those studies mostly used nutritionally designed shakes with set calories and protein. Homemade fruit smoothies are not the same thing. Two cautions:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Fullness.</strong> In a Penn State study, whole apples kept people fuller and cut meal intake more than applesauce or apple juice with the same calories (Flood-Obbagy &amp; Rolls, <em>Appetite</em>, 2009). Blended fruit may leave some people hungrier.</li>
<li><strong>Protein.</strong> Fruit-based smoothies can be low in protein. Adding Greek yogurt, milk, soy milk, or protein powder helps you stay full and protects muscle while you lose weight.</li>
</ul>
