Pick the right kind of video editor

"Video editor" covers very different skills. Searching for the exact job you need saves a lot of back-and-forth.

  • Short-form editors focus on Reels, TikTok, and Shorts: fast cuts, animated captions, trending pacing.
  • YouTube editors handle long talking-head videos, retention edits, B-roll, chapters, and sometimes thumbnails.
  • Podcast editors sync multiple cameras, clean audio, and cut clips from long episodes.
  • Ad editors build hook-first promos and export several sizes for paid campaigns.
  • Corporate and event editors work on interviews, training videos, weddings, and highlight reels.

How to vet a video editor in 10 minutes

  1. Watch the portfolio, not the thumbnail. Open two or three full samples. Do they look like the style you want? Is the audio clean? Are captions in sync?
  2. Match the format. A great wedding editor is not automatically a great TikTok editor. Look for samples in your exact format.
  3. Read recent reviews. Sort by most recent and look for buyers with projects similar to yours. Comments about communication and revisions tell you more than star ratings.
  4. Check the package details. Note the footage limit, finished length, number of revisions, delivery time, and whether source files or music licensing are included.
  5. Send a short message before ordering. Share your brief (template below) and ask for a quote. How clearly they reply is a preview of the whole project.

Red flags to skip

  • Asking to pay or talk outside Fiverr. Fiverr's terms say payments must go through the site. Moving off-platform removes your protection.
  • A portfolio full of stock reels with no client work, or samples that look identical across many different sellers.
  • Vague answers about revisions, delivery time, or what "unlimited" really means.
  • Prices far below the market for long, complex edits. A 20-minute polished video for $10 usually means corners are cut somewhere.
  • No questions about your project. Good editors ask about style, audience, and deadline before they start.
  • Unclear music rights. Ask where music comes from. Unlicensed tracks can get your video muted or claimed.

Video editing brief template (copy and paste)

Editors quote faster and more accurately when they know exactly what you want. Fill this in and send it in your first message.

PROJECT: [YouTube video / Reel / ad / podcast clip] RAW FOOTAGE: [total minutes, number of files, camera or phone] FINISHED LENGTH: [e.g. 8–10 minutes / 30 seconds] FORMAT: [16:9, 9:16, 1:1] and resolution [1080p / 4K] STYLE REFERENCE: [link to 1–2 videos you like + what you like about them] MUST INCLUDE: [captions, B-roll, zooms, lower thirds, logo intro, end screen] AUDIO: [noise cleanup? background music? who supplies music?] BRAND: [fonts, colors, logo files attached] DEADLINE: [date and time zone] REVISIONS: [how many rounds you expect] DELIVERABLES: [final MP4, project file?, thumbnail?] BUDGET: [your range] QUESTION: Can you share a sample similar to this style?

Step by step: placing your first order

  1. Search the Video Editing category and filter by budget and delivery time.
  2. Shortlist three editors whose samples match your format.
  3. Message each with the brief. Ask for a custom offer if your project does not fit a package.
  4. Choose the one with the clearest answer, not only the lowest price.
  5. Upload footage with clear file names and a list of timestamps you want kept or cut.
  6. Review the first cut carefully and send all feedback in one organized message with timestamps.
  7. Approve only when the final file matches the brief. Leave honest feedback.

FAQ

How much should I pay for a YouTube video edit on Fiverr?

It depends on length and effects. Fiverr's guide puts an average basic edit near $65, while longer YouTube edits with B-roll, captions, and color often cost $100 to $400 or more. Add Fiverr's 5.5% service fee, plus $3.50 under $200.

Can I ask for revisions?

Yes, within the number included in the package you buy. Check the revision count before ordering, and send all feedback for each round in one clear message.

Who owns the final video?

Fiverr's terms generally transfer rights in the delivered work to the buyer once it is paid for, unless the gig says otherwise. Ask about music and stock footage licenses separately, since those belong to third parties.

Is it safe to pay a video editor on Fiverr?

Paying through Fiverr is the safe route: the platform holds payment until the order is completed. Never pay outside Fiverr, even if a seller offers a discount.

How long does a video edit take?

Short clips often take one to three days. Long or complex edits can take a week or more. Rush delivery is usually a paid extra.
