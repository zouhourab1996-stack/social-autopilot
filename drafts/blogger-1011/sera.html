How Seraphina Tarot works, step by step

The funnel is designed to feel like a personal session. Here is the flow we saw on the public pages:

  1. Pick three cards for your Past, Present, and Future from a face-down spread.
  2. Enter your first name, then your email ("where should I send your complete reading?"). An optional box asks for your phone number for text alerts.
  3. Enter your birth date. The page shows your zodiac sign.
  4. Choose a focus: Love & Relationships, Money & Abundance, Purpose & Direction, or Health & Energy.
  5. A short "reading room" chat-style page builds anticipation, then you reach the sales page with the price.

Good to know: you share your name, email, and birthday before you ever see a price. That is common for this kind of offer, but it means you will likely get marketing emails even if you do not buy.

Is the reading really personal?

The marketing leans on a story of a grandmother's tradition and hand-crafted readings. We cannot verify that story. What we could see is that the public reading page appears to assemble its text from pre-written passages matched to your chosen cards, your zodiac sign, and your focus area, and then lets you download it as a PDF.

That is not a scam in itself. Many tarot apps work the same way. But it does mean two people who pick the same cards, sign, and focus will likely receive very similar text. If you want something written just for you, a live reader is a better fit.

A useful way to read any tarot product: treat it as a prompt for journaling, not as a forecast. If a line resonates, ask yourself why.
