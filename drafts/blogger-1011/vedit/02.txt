<p>You have the footage. Maybe it is a YouTube talk, a product demo, or a pile of phone clips for Reels. What you do not have is the time (or patience) to cut it. Fiverr is one of the fastest places to find a video editor, but the range is huge: some gigs start at $5, others at several hundred dollars.</p>

<p>This guide helps you hire well the first time. You will see what video editing usually costs on Fiverr, which red flags to skip, and a copy-paste brief template that makes editors quote accurately.</p>

<div class="pr-box pr-key">
<strong>Key takeaways</strong>
<ul>
<li><strong>Typical price:</strong> Fiverr's own cost guide puts an average basic edit at about $65. Short social clips cost less; long YouTube videos and ads cost more.</li>
<li><strong>Add the fee:</strong> Fiverr adds a 5.5% service fee to each payment, plus $3.50 on purchases under $200.</li>
<li><strong>Brief first, buy second:</strong> a clear brief (length, style, deadline, revisions) is the single best way to avoid a bad result.</li>
<li><strong>Test small:</strong> start with one video before you order a monthly batch.</li>
</ul>
