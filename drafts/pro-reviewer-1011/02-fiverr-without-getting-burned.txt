<p>Fiverr's own data says most buyers lose money exactly the same way: they buy big from a stranger. After verifying 150+ gigs across 14 categories for our <a href="https://trustscore.bond/reviews/">2026 buyer's guides</a>, the safe pattern is boringly consistent.</p>

<h2>1. The three-day window is your refund</h2>
<p>Fiverr auto-completes orders three days after delivery if you do nothing. Test everything inside that window — or you've approved it.</p>

<h2>2. Smallest package, every time</h2>
<p>Every category has an entry tier designed for exactly this: $5 readings, $10 audits, 2-hour VA trials. Judge the questions a seller asks before starting, not the polish of the delivery.</p>

<h2>3. Read the negative reviews for one word</h2>
<p>"Generic." In reviews for writers, readers, designers and translators alike, "generic" is the honest tell. Filter by lowest rating and read five.</p>

<h2>4. The upsell taxonomy</h2>
<p>Cheap readings discovering expensive "blockages". Logo gigs where the source file costs 3x. "Unlimited revisions" that exclude everything you'd want revised. Name the upsell before ordering and the price becomes honest.</p>

<h2>5. Own your accounts</h2>
<p>Automation on the freelancer's Zapier account is rent. Websites on the developer's hosting are hostages. Passwords in a manager, everything in your name, revoke on day one after handover.</p>
